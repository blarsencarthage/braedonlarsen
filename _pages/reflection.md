## Capstone Reflection

The goal of my portfolio is to provide a intuitive and approachable summary of who I am as a researcher and as a professional. I want to show who I am in a extended format that any potential employer, coworker, or other potential professional contact can take and digest who I am and what I have done. The biggest section I will want to include will be large projects and research, I want to inform the reader of both the hard research skills I have in particular fields, and in soft skills like project management, teamwork, document preparation, etc. Apart from projects, I also want to display where I have been and what I have done to get where I am, things to show how much I have worked and where I have worked to get to where I am.

So far, much of my work on my portfolio has been interchangeable with my work on graduate school applications, the overlap is primarily in determining who I am and what my next steps are, from there that constrains how I format documentation such as my resume and later on my portfolio. Apart from grad school specific tasks, my portfolio itself has involved a large amount of consolidation of my career as a college student to understand what specific works I have done and how they can transfer to my future career.

What I have learned from this work, is that specifically I know I want to pursue a PhD in alternative propulsion technologies for spacecraft. Specifically, researching and developing new methods for electric and plasma based propulsion. I know I want this, not because I want a title, but because I enjoy doing the hard tasks of research. I want to work at a problem that is incredibly difficult and have the freedom to ask questions and somewhat, learn what I want when I want. Given all these requirements I have determined the best course of action is to apply for PhD programs, and because of this decision that will greatly guide how I structure my portfolio.

I have now been majoring in physics (and some form of engineering) for a little over 3 years now, in that time, I have of course learned many concepts in and out of physics, and changed as a person to incorporate that new information. Many of these experiences were key points that I can cite as more significant changes in my understandings. Both how to think like a physicist and understanding "the plot" of academics and research or more simply, getting the wider picture of a problem and how every piece fits into the wider picture. I feel this perspective has prepared me well for working on complex problems and handling situations where my existing knowledge is limited.

Looking back on my freshman year, I specifically remember that my experience in physics was limited to my high-school experience in Science Olympiad and AP physics courses. Because of my lack of knowledge, I initially entered into college with the goal of a double B.S in physics and engineering. I quickly realized that, while mathematically possible, this course of action would not lead to a enjoyable college experience, nor would it work towards my greater goals. I knew early on that research, and very likely a PhD program or similar graduate education would be my next goal. With this realization and a year of engineering and physics courses completed, I took all of this into consideration and made the switch to be doing a B.S in Physics with a B.A in Applied Engineering, the first decision of mine that came from information I got from being a college student.

Having made this decision I have then changed my view to work towards understanding how to succeed beyond a classroom. This has lead to me spending much of my remaining career as a undergraduate in various research projects. Which culminated in my research into data analysis techniques for the MUTT project under Dr. Kevin Crosby, which has given me the opportunity to preform original research in a unique topic.

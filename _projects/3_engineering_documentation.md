Throughout my time at Carthage I have stood out as a double major in physics and applied engineering. This portfolio discusses how working within a physics degree has shaped me, but equally as important to the person I am nearing the end of college, is my work in engineering. There have been several impactful experiences in engineering that have taught me lessons I would have never learned in physics. The first major experience that comes to my mind is the Engineering Practice courses taught by Dr. Lavorata.

This series of two classes was designed to be a comprehensive engineering project experience from project proposal, to a final prototype design. While there were many lessons I learned in project planning and management, the biggest lesson I learned was documentation. In engineering, there is commonly a several large reports and presentations that are given throughout the project development cycle given to stakeholders to ensure that you are keeping to some standard of what they envision for the project. The ease in preparing these reports is directly correlated to how well your group has maintained strict and large quantities of notes regarding design decisions, calculations, and general thought processes.

I have found that in research, depending on the environment, projects can get less structured and more chaotic. In such a environment, there is less motivation to keep up with documentation practices, which works while you remember the relevant information. Because of this, I make it my effort to document thoroughly and effectively, that way, beyond my own time on a particular project, my work is remembered and accessible to others. As well, it ensures my thinking process meets a level of external standards without needing to even present to others, having to write out processes or ideas can lead to a different perspective in sanity-checking than when the idea is in your brain. That is why, in any research environment, the ideas of engineering documentation can slow you down in some ways, but help ensure you never have to backtrack over yourself.

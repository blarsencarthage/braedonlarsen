## Reflection

Throughout my time at Carthage, one time that I am very fortunate to have gotten a lot of experience with, is my in getting oral presentations. Many of these presentations were given in classes either as a oral presentation on a topic, or as a lecture given to the class on a topic.

My sophomore year I was taking PHY2300 Modern Physics, one of the final deliverables for this course was a 20 minute in-class lecture delivered on a modern physics topic, not covered in class. We were expected to go out and independently research, comprehend, and prepare this lecture. My particular topic was a conceptual understand of the nuclear physics necessary in order to understand how nuclear fusion works.

This project was significant, as it was my first major independent solo project with the primary goal of teaching a topic to a room full of people. From both preparing and giving this lecture, I was better able to understand the differences between simply knowing a topic and being able to teach it. Being able to teach a topic requires a far more comprehensive understanding than if you are learning a topic yourself or in a class. You need to understand the topic on a "general" or abstract level, which will allow you to use multiple different metaphors and examples to teach and explain with. Which is opposed to how I used to learn more complicated topics, my knowledge would be reliant on metaphors with my thinking of the topic rigid to how I learned it. Instead, pushing myself to "learn better" leads to me disconnecting my understandings from those metaphors.

Developing a elevator pitch really makes me think about what is truly relevant to others when they are trying to get a understanding of me. The goal of the elevator pitch is both simple and complex: it is both getting a person to be interested in my story, and be consequence getting to know me better. How can I get that person whether that being a potential graduate school mentor, a potential employer, someone I'm networking with, or a peer level social connection. The elevator pitch is like creating a story in which the goal is expressing who you are and what you "stand" for in whatever context the conversation carries.

Initially, I tried to inject as much personality into my elevator pitch as possible, however I quickly found that the more personality, in many ways, the more flat and less interesting the rest of my information became as a consequence. I think because I am naturally a very sarcastic and humorous person, which can work well to get people to pay attention for the humorous portions, but then the tone shift to a serious explanation of my skills is boring and inconsistent. However, my pitch can be completely uninteresting and boring if I go too far in the other direction and make it a simple repetition of my skills ands previous work. All of that information is on a resume/CV, therefore anyone interested in me in a professional context would find that information very easily.

This conclusion was the most important "discovery" I made about making an elevator pitch. I need to find the balance of being interesting and engaging, but also not casual. I think my final idea for an elevator pitch reflects this, I don't necessarily just list what I've done, it is instead about why I want to pursue furthered education. I found it effective to tell it in the format of a story. What have I done to get to where I am (which gives me a chance to mention some of my acheivements quickly), and why have these experiences benefited me. I can explain exactly what I find interesting about my past research experience and depending on the context of who I talking to, this is where I can engage them to talk. If there are a potential grad school mentor, I can leverage my knowledge in my research field to ask intelligent questions about their work. And for other people, I can ask them about their work.

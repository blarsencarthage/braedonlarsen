## My Pitch

Hello, I am Braedon Larsen, I'm finishing degrees in Physics and Applied Engineering from Carthage College, being in my senior year, I've had to think about what my next steps are. For several years I've known I have wanted to go to graduate school, particularly because I have found I enjoy research (being a large open-ended problem), and because I love to be challenged with new concepts, particularly by logic and math problems. I've found this love for research from the projects I have worked on throughout my undergrad where I have worked on many different projects both as a researcher during the school year in Carthage's Microgravity Lab, and at NASA's JSC in my two OSTEM internships. All of this research has been in the general field of microgravity fluid mechanics and the concepts I have had to learn for these projects have given me ideas for what I could want to do or a PhD. I like the nature of working in continuum mechanics and in classical field theory, expressing systems in those terms just makes sense. This is coupled with my time at JSC where I was a propulsion intern, this deepened my enjoyment for the behaviors of plasma, which have much of the same underlying mechanics and principles as working with fluids in microgravity.

## Reflection

Developing a elevator pitch really makes me think about what is truly relevant to others when they are trying to get a understanding of me. The goal of the elevator pitch is both simple and complex: it is both getting a person to be interested in my story, and be consequence getting to know me better. How can I get that person whether that being a potential graduate school mentor, a potential employer, someone I'm networking with, or a peer level social connection. The elevator pitch is like creating a story in which the goal is expressing who you are and what you "stand" for in whatever context the conversation carries. Initially, I tried to inject as much personality into my elevator pitch as possible, however I quickly found that the more personality, in many ways, the more flat and less interesting the rest of my information became as a consequence. I think because I am naturally a very sarcastic and humorous person, which can work well to get people to pay attention for the humorous portions, but then the tone shift to a serious explanation of my skills is boring and inconsistent. However, my pitch can be completely uninteresting and boring if I go too far in the other direction and make it a simple repetition of my skills ands previous work. All of that information is on a resume/CV, therefore anyone interested in me in a professional context would find that information very easily. This conclusion was the most important "discovery" I made about making an elevator pitch. I need to find the balance of being interesting and engaging, but also not casual. I think my final idea for an elevator pitch reflects this, I don't necessarily just list what I've done, it is instead about why I want to pursue furthered education. I found it effective to tell it in the format of a story. What have I done to get to where I am (which gives me a chance to mention some of my acheivements quickly), and why have these experiences benefited me. I can explain exactly what I find interesting about my past research experience and depending on the context of who I talking to, this is where I can engage them to talk. If there are a potential grad school mentor, I can leverage my knowledge in my research field to ask intelligent questions about their work. And for other people, I can ask them about their work.

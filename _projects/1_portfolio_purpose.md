The goal of my portfolio is to provide a intuitive and approachable summary of who I am as a researcher and as a professional. I want to show who I am in a extended format that any potential employer, coworker, or other potential professional contact can take and digest who I am and what I have done. The biggest section I will want to include will be large projects and research, I want to inform the reader of both the hard research skills I have in particular fields, and in soft skills like project management, teamwork, document preparation, etc. Apart from projects, I also want to display where I have been and what I have done to get where I am, things to show how much I have worked and where I have worked to get to where I am.

So far, much of my work on my portfolio has been interchangeable with my work on graduate school applications, the overlap is primarily in determining who I am and what my next steps are, from there that constrains how I format documentation such as my resume and later on my portfolio. Apart from grad school specific tasks, my portfolio itself has involved a large amount of consolidation of my career as a college student to understand what specific works I have done and how they can transfer to my future career.

What I have learned from this work, is that specifically I know I want to pursue a PhD in alternative propulsion technologies for spacecraft. Specifically, researching and developing new methods for electric and plasma based propulsion. I know I want this, not because I want a title, but because I enjoy doing the hard tasks of research. I want to work at a problem that is incredibly difficult and have the freedom to ask questions and somewhat, learn what I want when I want. Given all these requirements I have determined the best course of action is to apply for PhD programs, and because of this decision that will greatly guide how I structure my portfolio.
